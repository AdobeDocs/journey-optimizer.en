# AI Knowledge Reference

+++ AI Knowledge Reference

* **TL;DR:** Explains how the personalized optimization model uses machine learning to learn from customer, offer, and contextual data to serve personalized offers and maximize KPIs, covering its use cases, dataset requirements, features, ensemble model components, assumptions, cold-start behavior, and re-training.

**Glossary:**

* **Personalized optimization** *(product-specific)*: Uses supervised machine learning and deep learning to let a business user (marketer) define business goals and use customer data to train business-oriented models that serve personalized offers and maximize KPIs.
* **Feature**: An input into the model. The model learns complex feature interactions between offers, users' information, and contextual information to recommend personalized offers to end users. There are 4 types of features: Decisioning objects, Audiences, Profile features, and Context features.
* **Decisioning objects** *(product-specific)*: Feature type made of placementID, activityID, and decisionScopeID. Part of the decision management feedback Experience Events sent to AEP.
* **Audiences**: Feature type.
* **Profile features** *(product-specific, Limited Availability)*: Feature type made of profile attributes from your profile dataset schema.
* **Context features** *(product-specific, Limited Availability)*: Feature type made of real-time context signals sent with the decision API request. Automatic context features (like channel ID) are always included. Pre-selected recommended context features (like device type, country code) can be removed. At decision time, the model uses the context values together with the profile's attributes and audience memberships to score the eligible offers in real time.
* **Offline model training**: Phase in which a model is trained by learning and memorizing feature interactions in historical data.
* **Online inference**: Phase in which candidate offers are ranked based on real-time scores generated by the model. Unlike traditional collaborative filtering techniques, with which it is hard to include features for users and offers, personalized optimization is a deep learning based recommendation method, and is able to include and learn complex and nonlinear feature interaction patterns.
* **Binary metric**: A metric such as clicks or conversions. Predicted values are always between 0 and 1.
* **Continuous metric**: A metric such as order value. Predicted values are always a number greater than or equal to zero. The model supports optimization of continuous variables such as revenue and customer lifetime value.
* **Ranking scores**: Normalized to ensure consistent behavior across both metric types when used in formulas or comparisons. They can be used directly in AI Model Formula builder expressions.
* **Ensemble model** *(product-specific)*: Personalized optimization is delivered as an ensemble model: several complementary model arms run together, and a supervisory layer decides how much live traffic each arm receives. The ensemble manages the balance between exploration and exploitation automatically by holding a minimum exploration floor while shifting the remaining traffic toward the better-performing personalized arms over time. It is composed of four traffic arms.
* **Exploration and exploitation**: Exploration is exploring under-tested offers to gather information and learn which offers perform best. Exploitation is exploiting proven offers to maximize immediate return. Reserving too little traffic for exploration leaves high-potential offers undiscovered; reserving too much sacrifices lift on offers that are already performing.
* **Uniform random (exploration arm)**: Assigns offers to customers at random from among the eligible offers. Because it does not favor any offer, it produces unbiased data about how customers respond across the full catalog, which is the raw material the personalized arms learn from.
* **Neural network (personalized arm)**: Predicts the best offer for a given customer based on their attributes and audience memberships. It learns complex, nonlinear interactions among offers, customer features, and context, and is well suited to capturing subtle patterns across many features.
* **Contextual bandit (personalized arm)**: A second personalized arm that also predicts the best offer for each customer based on their attributes, audience memberships, and context, using a bandit approach that continually balances learning and performance as it serves. Running it alongside the neural network lets the ensemble draw on the strengths of two distinct personalized methods.
* **New offer booster (non-personalized arm)** *(product-specific)*: An overall-winner Thompson Sampling bandit (non-personalized) that makes optimistic assumptions about the performance of new offers, those with few recorded impression events within the model lookback period. It gives promising new offers early exposure and addresses a known cold-start shortcoming in which the model otherwise struggled to direct enough traffic to new or high-performing but restrictively eligible offers. As true impression and conversion data is collected, each offer's estimated performance quickly approaches its true underlying performance, and the impact of the optimistic assumptions falls to near zero. When no offers are relatively new (for example, when all offers have a similar number of impressions, or all have more than 1,000 impressions), the optimistic effect is near zero and this arm behaves, in effect, as a non-personalized overall-winner model.
* **Cold-start problem**: Occurs when there is not enough data to make recommendations. For personalized optimization, there are five types of cold-start cases:
    * After creating a new AI model with no historical data, offers are randomly served for a period of time to collect the required data, which is then used to train the first model.
    * After the first AI model is released, a portion of total traffic is allocated for uniform random exploration while the remainder is used for model recommendations. The traffic distribution across the exploration and exploitation bandit components is tuned automatically based on factors such as the number of offers and their performance thresholds.
    * After new offers are added to the offer collection selected in the strategy associated with the AI ranking model, those offers become eligible candidates for exploration by both the uniform random and new offer booster model arms (within 60 minutes). During the next scheduled re-training run, the offer's estimated performance is updated in the new offer booster model arm, and the offer becomes eligible for inclusion in the personalized model arms if it met the impression and click threshold.
    * After new profiles are added to the existing audience set associated with the selection strategy associated with the AI ranking model, they inherit personalization attributes from the audience set itself, and start receiving personalized offers based on those attributes from the get-go, without any cold-start problem.
    * After new audience sets, profile features, and/or context features are added to the existing model, the data associated with them starts to get collected and is used from the next scheduled re-training run.
* **Re-training**: Models are re-trained weekly to learn latest feature interactions and mitigate model performance degradation.

**Guardrails:**

* To train a personalized optimization model, the dataset must have at least two offers with at least 250 display events (for example, impressions) and one success event (for example, click or conversion) each within the last 30 days.
* Offers with fewer than 250 display events and/or no success events within the last 30 days remain eligible for inclusion in the exploration traffic and new offer booster traffic. They are also eligible for inclusion in the personalization traffic but are treated as equivalent to the worst scoring predicted offer in decisioning, until they meet the required minimum display/success events and the model gets re-trained.
* Audiences: 0-50 audiences can be added as features when creating or editing the Ranking AI model.
* Profile features (Limited Availability): 0-50 can be added as features when creating or editing the Ranking AI model. If applicable, DULE policies apply to selected profile features automatically.
* Context features (Limited Availability): 0-20 additional context features can be added when creating or editing the Ranking AI model.
* Datatypes of profile features and context features (Limited Availability): numeric, string, Enum, and binary. Datetime, array, and key-value pair datatypes are not available.
* Traffic per arm at initialization and after the first successful training run:
    * Uniform random arm: at initialization, 100% of traffic (it is the only arm active before the first model is trained); after the first successful training run, a minimum of 10% of traffic, up to a maximum of 85% (depending on the number of observed impression and conversion events per offer).
    * Neural network arm: at initialization, 0% of traffic; after the first successful training run, a minimum of 5% of traffic, up to a maximum of 80%.
    * Contextual bandit arm: at initialization, 0% of traffic; after the first successful training run, a minimum of 5% of traffic, up to a maximum of 80%.
    * New offer booster arm: at initialization, 0% of traffic; after the first successful training run, 5% of traffic.
    * Actual traffic allocation will vary based on observed arm performance.
* You are responsible for ensuring that data used to train Personalized optimization models, including profile, audience, and context features, is appropriate for your use case and complies with applicable laws and regulations. Sensitive or protected information, and attributes or audience memberships that proxy for it, may introduce or amplify bias in model outcomes.
* Key assumptions and limitations of personalized optimization:
    * Offers are different enough so that users have different preferences among the offers in consideration. If offers are too similar, a resulting model has less impact as the responses are seemingly random.
    * User traffic composition is stable. If user traffic composition changes dramatically during model training and predicting, model performance could degrade.
    * Offers performances do not change dramatically over a short period of time, as this model updates weekly and changes to performance are conveyed as the model updates.

**Prerequisites and permissions:**

* Profile features and context features: currently available as a Limited Availability release. Contact your Adobe representative to gain access.
* Context features (Limited Availability): if your model uses context features, the decision request must carry the values for those features. Send them with the Web SDK or Mobile SDK, as configured in your datastream.

**Applicability:**

* Context features (Limited Availability): only available for inbound channels.

**Terminology:**

* Canonical name: Personalized optimization model
* Acronym: AEP — Adobe Experience Platform
* Do not confuse: "Personalized optimization" ≠ "non-personalized ranking". Non-personalized ranking optimizes based on the global performance of each offer, while Personalized optimization learns the relationship between an individual customer's attributes and the offers most likely to drive the chosen KPI for that customer, so the offer selection is tailored to each profile rather than a single best offer served to everyone.

**FAQ:**

* **What are the key benefits of Personalized optimization?** It maximizes the business KPI you select, continuously adapts as new interaction data arrives, supports both binary and continuous optimization metrics, and reduces the manual effort of A/B testing and rules authoring.
* **What are the dataset requirements to train a personalized optimization model?** The dataset must have at least two offers with at least 250 display events and one success event each within the last 30 days.
* **What happens before the first personalized optimization model is trained?** Offers within a selection strategy utilizing a personalized optimization model are served at random.
* **Which feature types can be added to the model?** Decisioning objects, Audiences, Profile features (Limited Availability), and Context features (Limited Availability).
* **How often are models re-trained?** Weekly.
* **What happens when new offers are added to the offer collection selected in the strategy?** They become eligible candidates for exploration by both the uniform random and new offer booster model arms (within 60 minutes).

**Examples:**

* Use cases of Personalized optimization, which is well suited to decisioning scenarios where different customers respond differently to the available offers and where the catalog of offers is meaningfully differentiated and does not change often:
    * Next-best-offer selection: choosing which of several competing offers or promotions to present to each customer in real time.
    * Content personalization: choosing which piece of content (for example, banner, creative) or message for each customer across web, mobile, email, and other channels.
    * Audience-aware personalization: incorporating audience membership, profile attributes, and contextual signals so that recommendations reflect who the customer is and the context of the interaction.
    * Revenue and value optimization: optimizing toward continuous outcomes such as revenue or customer lifetime value, in addition to binary outcomes such as clicks and conversions.
* Illustrative example, binary response (conversion): a simplified dataset of historical interactions between users and offers. Each row records an offer that was shown, two customer signals (loyalty tier, high = 1; whether the customer opened a recent email, yes = 1), and whether the customer converted (yes = 1). For Offer A, conversion is more likely when both signals agree (both high or both low). For Offer B, conversion is more likely when the email was opened, regardless of loyalty tier. Based on the learned pattern, the model can predict the better offer for each customer based on their signals.
* Illustrative example, continuous response (revenue): instead of predicting whether a customer converts, the model predicts a continuous value (expected revenue) for each offer and customer segment, and ranks offers by that predicted value. For high-loyalty customers who opened the email, Offer A is expected to drive the most revenue; for low-loyalty customers who opened the email, Offer B is the stronger choice.
* Example for the first assumption: if a bank has two credit card offers with the only difference being color, it may not matter which card is recommended; if each card has different terms, this provides rationale for why certain customers would choose one.
* Example for the second assumption: if only data for users in audience A is available in the model training phase, but the trained model is used to generate predictions for users in audience B, model performance could be impacted.
* Example for the third assumption: a product was very popular before, but a public report identifies the product to be harmful to our health, and the product becomes unpopular extremely fast. In this scenario, the model could continue to predict this product until the model updates with changes in user behavior.
